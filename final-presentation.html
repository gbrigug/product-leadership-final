The case

Five deliverables, one strategy

Each lab committed one decision. Together they are the bet, the sequence, the team that owns it, the money, and what you learned building it.

Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Immediate access to directly solve any mental health or wellbeing issues is in the palm of my hand via the Fable app.

The hard no

- Your one hard no: I will not make our existing core problem solving techniques/experiences purposefully less effective because it's a disservice to our users and will impact the company's reputation.

Objective and key results

- Objective: Fable can address new markets with it's expanded content & self-help material.
- KR1: Modular activation rate (users completing their first resolution module within 48 hours) increases from X% to Y%.
- KR2: Repeat 30-day user behavior grows 5% with the release of new content
- KR3: Self-reported symptom reduction / issue-resolution rate after completing a content track increases by 20% over baseline.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

Sequenced First (NOW): Onboarding friction removal and habit-building loops. Prioritizing activation and retention first ensures we plug top-of-funnel leaks before scaling content production.

Pushed Out (NEXT): Broad content expansion, self-reported symptom reduction tracking (KR3), and formal competitor gap analysis. These require a baseline of active, retained users to yield accurate data and maximize content ROI.

Trade-off memo

Cut / De-prioritized for Later (LATER): Real-time algorithmic personalization and B2B institutional scaling. Deep personalization and enterprise channels add high operational complexity and are deprioritized until direct-to-consumer efficacy is proven.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

The core audio/visual player, multi-session resolution tracks, and in-app efficacy tracking that guide users from acute stress through post-module progress.

How we decide

PM's define & own strategy in their respective domains. Team can be consulted to provide input. Overlap situations require peer input & joint decision within 48 hours, else Product Leaders are brought in to help drive decisions. Product leaders will give verdict in 24 hours.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

By redesigning the onboarding flow, we'll capture 50% of the existing 40% drop-off. This will result in a larger user base, more eligible subscribers, and a reduction in churn.

Kill criterion

If the signup abandon rate does not drop by 35% in 60 days, no further investment will be made in redesigning the initial flow. Existing remaining capital allocation will be canceled. Engineering effort will be allocated to other initiatives to reduce churn.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

I thought the negotiation exercise was the most difficult, but also the most fruitful. Any exchange with different competing goals and priorities is tough, as you need to find alignment. Preparing to frame my approach a particular manner, and knowing what I can compromise on, helps significantly in achieving a helpful outcome.

Key learnings

The focus on converting product outcomes to financial outputs was the most impactful personally. It's not enough to drive product metrics; I need to drive actual P/L value.

Aha moment

In the world of AI, pressure testing my strategy immediately with pre-defined prompts was helpful. I could have a critical eye review my work without taking up time from others and in a real-time manner. It helps to remove bias, add clarity, and close gaps before presenting it outwards.
